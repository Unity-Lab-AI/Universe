# Against the field

> The honest comparison: Universe beside every well-known program that makes or shows a universe, and the two tiny pioneers that showed the way. Each project's own published numbers; sizes are what each asks for on disk. Where a claim is checked, its source is linked.

---

## Size and scope

| | On disk | What it holds | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Universe** | **1.7 MB** | About 10²² stars in about 100 billion galaxies, every world down to its grains, atoms and quarks, and on below them, in one unbroken zoom | Everything grown from one seed and 0.26 MB of templates; no stored art, no sound |
| [Noctis IV](https://archive.org/details/msdos_noctis_shareware "Noctis IV on the Internet Archive") (2002) | About 1 to 5 MB | One galaxy of about 78 billion stars, with planets to walk on | Grown from a seed, by one person, for DOS |
| [Elite](https://www.theregister.com/2024/01/18/elite_game_retro/ "Elite at 40, The Register") (1984) | 22 KB | Eight galaxies of 256 planets | Grown from a seed: the first game to do it |
| [.kkrieger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.kkrieger ".kkrieger on Wikipedia") (2004) | 96 KB | One shooter level, about ten minutes of play (not a universe: the famous size benchmark) | Textures, models and music grown at start-up |
| [SpaceEngine](https://store.steampowered.com/app/314650/SpaceEngine/ "SpaceEngine on Steam") | 4 GB, 50 GB with its HD packs | Billions of galaxies of billions of stars, down to a world's ground; 130,000 real catalogued objects | Grown from position, plus catalogues and textures |
| [Elite Dangerous](https://www.elitedangerous.com/explore "Elite Dangerous: Explore") | 25 GB (75 GB with Odyssey) | One galaxy, 400 billion star systems | Stellar Forge grows systems from first principles, plus art and sound |
| [No Man's Sky](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/no-mans-skys-18-quintillion-planets-take-up-just-6/1100-6441663/ "GameSpot, 2016: 18 quintillion planets in 6 GB") | 6 GB at launch (mostly sound), 10 to 15 GB now | 18 quintillion planets | Grown from a 64-bit seed, plus art and sound |
| [Universe Sandbox](https://universesandbox.com/faq/ "Universe Sandbox FAQ") | 4 GB | A system of bodies at a time, under gravity | A physics toy, not a universe to fly through |
| [Celestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestia "Celestia on Wikipedia") 1.6 | About 36 MB | 118,000 real stars and the known worlds | Catalogues, nothing grown |
| [Pioneer](https://sourceforge.net/projects/pioneer-space-simulator.mirror/files/20260907/ "Pioneer downloads") | About 550 MB | A galaxy of millions of systems to fly and trade in | Grown from a seed, plus art and sound |

## What each needs, and how Universe runs

| | Needs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Universe** | Windows 10 or 11, any Direct3D 12 card. No install size to speak of (1.7 MB), no download beyond the 0.6 MB installer | Every pixel is worked out on the card from equations, so the card does the work; a card with little memory starts at a lower render scale by itself, and **View > GPU use** holds the card at the share you choose (10 to 90%) by turning the detail up or down. On the lab's machine (an RTX 4070 Ti Super at 3840 by 2024, the stats on screen): 60 frames a second among the galaxies and at a star's worlds, 45 to 60 among the atoms, 19 to 26 on a world's grains while the ground under the camera is still being made |
| [SpaceEngine](https://store.steampowered.com/app/314650/SpaceEngine/ "SpaceEngine on Steam") | GTX 1050 Ti or RX 460 with 3 GB of card memory, 6 GB of RAM, 4 GB of disk (recommended: GTX 1060, 8 GB, 50 GB) | The nearest in scope: galaxies to a world's ground, with textures for the real worlds |
| [Universe Sandbox](https://universesandbox.com/faq/ "Universe Sandbox FAQ") | A Direct3D 12 card with 4 GB of card memory, 4 GB of RAM, 4 GB of disk | Simulates a few hundred bodies at once, not a universe |
| [Elite Dangerous](https://www.pcgamesn.com/elite-dangerous/system-requirements "Elite Dangerous system requirements, PCGamesN") | GTX 470 or R7 240, 6 GB of RAM, 25 GB of disk; Odyssey: GTX 780 with 3 GB, 8 GB of RAM, 75 GB | A full game with ships, trade, missions and sound |
| [No Man's Sky](https://www.pcgamesn.com/no-mans-sky/system-requirements "No Man's Sky system requirements, PCGamesN") | GTX 1060 with 3 GB, RX 470, or Intel UHD 630 graphics; 8 GB of RAM; 15 GB of disk | A full game, with art and sound, that now runs on built-in graphics |
| [Celestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestia "Celestia on Wikipedia") 1.6 | Any computer of the last twenty years | Catalogues drawn as they are |
| [Pioneer](https://wiki.pioneerspacesim.net/wiki/FAQ "Pioneer FAQ") | An OpenGL 3.2 card, about 550 MB of disk | Open source, Windows and Linux |
| [Noctis IV](https://archive.org/details/msdos_noctis_shareware "Noctis IV on the Internet Archive") | A DOS PC of the late 1990s | Software rendering, 320 by 200 |
| [Elite](https://www.theregister.com/2024/01/18/elite_game_retro/ "Elite at 40, The Register") (1984) | A BBC Micro with 32 KB of memory | Wireframe |

## Where it stands

Nothing in these tables goes from the cosmic web to a quark in one unbroken camera, and nothing but the two tiny pioneers comes near the size. Noctis is the closest in spirit: one person, one galaxy, a few megabytes, twenty years ago. SpaceEngine is the closest in scope, more than two thousand times the size, and it stops at the ground.

## To be fair

The others carry what Universe doesn't yet: sound, stored art and textures, gameplay, ships, trade, missions, years of polish. Universe is a 0.1 preview with no life on its worlds yet. Its size is small because every pixel is worked out on the graphics card from equations, so it needs a Direct3D 12 card and does its work there; the frame rate at the grains, where the ground under the camera is made finer and finer as you come down, is the one place it still waits on the processor.
