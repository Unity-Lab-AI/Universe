# How to use it

> **Starting up:** the universe is made before it opens, with a bar showing how far along it is (a few seconds; the controls wait till it's ready).
>
> **New here?** Press **T** for the grand tour, a film from the whole universe down to the quarks. A click, the wheel or a key pauses it, and you can fly it yourself from there.

## Flying

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mouse wheel** | Fly toward what the mouse points at. You slow as you come close, so you never crash and never stop |
| **Ctrl + wheel** | Eight times faster |
| **W A S D** | Forward, back, left and right, at a speed that suits what is nearest |
| **Q / E** | Down and up |
| **Shift** | Hold to fly four times faster |
| **Left click** | Pick a galaxy, star, world, spot on the ground, grain or atom. The camera turns to it and the wheel flies you there |
| **Right click** | Let go of what you picked |
| **Drag** | With something picked, circle round it. With nothing picked, look around |
| **B** | Among atoms: glide across the bond to the next atom |

## Time

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Space** | Pause time, or carry on |
| **,** and **.** | Slower and faster, from real time to ten years a second (it starts at a minute a second) |
| **Time menu** | The same speeds, **Always day** (on to start with) to keep every world in daylight; turn it off for true day and night; and **Gravity**: turn it off and every body goes straight on at its speed, every star is blown apart, a galaxy's stars fly off |

Every world turns on its own day, so you can sit on the ground and watch its star rise and set.

## The grand tour

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **T** | Start the tour, or carry a paused tour on from where it was |
| **R** | Start a paused tour again from the beginning |
| **Backspace** | Close the *Tour paused* note |

A **click**, the **wheel** or a **key** pauses the tour, and a note on screen says so and why. Shift, Ctrl, Alt, the Windows keys and shortcuts such as **Alt+Tab** or **Win+Shift+S** never stop it. Every viewing is shot afresh, so no two tours are the same.

## Pictures and clips

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **F12** | A screenshot (PNG) |
| **F9** | Start or stop recording a clip (its size chosen on the landing page or under View > Clip size; a smaller clip's text is drawn larger, so it reads): one MP4 for as long as it records, 30 frames a second, packed by quality so black space costs nothing and the stars stay sharp (a few tens of MB a minute at 1080p; a 15-minute tour at half of a 4K screen is about 570 MB). View > Clip size chooses the clip's size against the screen: auto (full up to 1920 wide, half above that), full, half (about a quarter of the file) or quarter (about a sixteenth). While it records a red REC with the clip's length blinks at the top of the screen, as on a camcorder; it stays out of the clip |

Both go in the `captures` folder. On the landing page, tick **Film the tour** to record the whole tour. The **Clips** list plays what you have filmed, and **Open folder** shows where it is.

## On screen

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **H** | Show or hide the scale bar and the info cards (a click that picks something brings them back) |
| **Info cards** | Whatever you pick gets a card: what it is and its facts (a star's heat and worlds, a world's orbit and year, the ground's height) |
| **View menu** | The periodic table (all 118 elements, each as it really is at the temperature and pressure you set; Escape comes back), drag speed, invert either drag direction, FPS, the GPU and CPU stats (the processor's and the card's time a frame, the processor's share of every core, the memory in use, the card's memory and how busy it is), render scale (lower is faster, higher is sharper), GPU use, and the clip's size (auto, full, half or quarter of the screen) |
| **Start page** | Back to the landing page |
| **Landing page** | Its tick boxes: frames a second, the GPU and CPU stats, invert up and down, drag speed, film the tour |
| **Help menu** | Every document, laid out to read. **Documents: what's here** lists them all, what each is made of and when to read it |
| **Esc** or **Exit** | Close |

## What you will see

1. **Galaxies**, each of its own kind. Close to one, its own stars fade in, made round you as you go. At its heart a giant black hole, feeding or quiet: the tour flies by a feeding one, round its disc, down to its horizon and through it, falling in black down the gravity well itself to the singularity, and out again.
2. **A supernova**, if one is going in the galaxies round you, else the youngest remnant: the fireball in its ballooning cloud, or the shell, and what was born at its middle.
3. **A star's system**: its worlds, moons, belts and the star itself; an asteroid of the belt and a comet by its tail, seen close.
4. **A world**: its real ground, seas, clouds and air. Plates, volcanoes, quakes, weather, ice ages and impacts run on its own clock. Its seas are dived into, down to the bed.
5. **Below the ground**: grains, the crystal of their mineral, then atoms, electrons, the nucleus and its quarks, one electron ridden beside like a comet.

Everything picked has its card at the left: what it is, what it is made of down to the traces, the numbers that matter.
