Quick guide — in plain English (the “for dummies” version)

What is this site, in one sentence? An artist drew hundreds of pictures by hand; we turned each picture into pure mathematics (a recipe of numbers), and your browser uses that recipe to redraw the picture, animate a world from it, and even play it as music. No actual picture files are stored — only the maths.

  1. Where the art comes from. Every piece is by one artist — a person with schizophrenia and a photographic memory who remembered the visions they saw and drew them by hand. This site captures the hidden mathematics inside those drawings.
  2. “Equations, not images” — what that actually means. A normal gallery saves photos (JPEG/PNG — grids of coloured dots). We save none of that. Instead each picture is stored as a long list of simple waves (called wavelets): each wave has a size, a place, and a strength. Add all the waves together and the picture appears. Think of it like a recipe instead of a finished cake — we ship the recipe, and your browser bakes the cake every time you look.
  3. The viewer (rebuild the actual picture). Open any piece and your browser solves its recipe to draw the real artwork at full quality. A “deviation” slider lets you throw away the weakest waves and watch the picture get rougher — proof there's no stored photo, just maths. You can also download the whole equation as a text file, and step through every piece with the ←/→ keys.
  4. The world engine (a living, morphing world). The video page takes each picture's maths, splits it into layers (the base picture plus its glow, structure, colours, edges and fine detail), stacks them, and slowly melts one piece into the next forever — not a slideshow. Sliders let you remix the colours, blend modes and motion, save your favourite settings, and record the result as a video or GIF.
  5. Zoom in & the equation fractalizes. Scroll to zoom into a piece on the viewer or the world engine (on the world engine it holds the morph while you zoom) — it stays the real image until you pass its native detail, then it fractalizes: the piece's own equation (Newton z³ + the cubic x³−2y³=3, with sine/cosine navigators) grows new self-similar detail that propagates out of the image's own edges and vectors — in the picture's own palette colours, infinitely — it never bottoms out. The detail grows along the structure, not a wash over the top.
  6. The music (every picture becomes a song). The same numbers that draw a picture also play as music. Big, coarse shapes become low/bass notes; tiny details become high notes. Nothing is a pre-made tune — the song is the picture's own maths. Each piece sounds different, and you can shape the instrument (guitar, violin, bell…), tempo and bass, then save the music as an audio file.
  7. Make your own. On Your equations you can drop in any image and it becomes its own equation entirely inside your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere — saved on your device to replay, morph and hear.
  8. Two heads-ups. (1) Because there's no stored picture, right-click “save image” is switched off — the real thing to take away is the equation. (2) All of this is heavy maths that needs a real computer with a graphics chip (GPU); phones can't run the engines, so on a phone you'll see all the writing but not the live picture/world/music.

That's the whole site in plain words. Everything below is the deeper, technical version — and the complete reference defines every symbol used in every formula.

🧠 The .uvme file — the equational file type

Shorthand (the whole thing in 30 seconds).
  • What it is: a .uvme is a real, standard WAV audio file that plays in any media player — with the artwork's full equation (the shared wavelet coefficient field C) tucked inside it as data. One file = the sound AND the equation.
  • What it does: from that one equation, our code regenerates the picture, the music, and the fractal — it is a Rosetta Stone. One master call reads it out as equation · image · png · jpg · webp · wav · sound · eqsound · fractal.
  • How you use it: play it anywhere like any .wav (rename to .wav if an app is fussy); rebuild the image by dropping it on the Combiner in a desktop browser. Make one from any image or sound on the Combiner / Your equations.
  • The honest catch: a file's first bytes can only declare one format, so on disk it opens natively as a .wav — the true “plays-everything” polyglot is the equation + code, not the container. The exact full image rides as data; the audible music alone isn't reversible (see below).
Full ins-and-outs follow; the symbol-level maths is in the complete reference, and the areas where it out-competes a stored image (lossless, multi-modal, resolution-free) are on the Proven page.

What's actually in the file (anatomy)

A .uvme is a bog-standard RIFF/WAVE container — the same format as any .wav — carrying:

  • The audible audio (the fmt  + data chunks): the pleasant sonification of the piece (the master music equation), real PCM you can listen to in any player.
  • A custom UVME chunk: the serialized coefficient field C = {(rk,ck,vk)} + palette + dimensions, the complete equation. It is CRC-32 checked, stamped with a schema version (uvm-combiner@1), and carries a produces manifest listing every representation it can become. Audio players skip unknown chunks (exactly how broadcast WAV carries its bext metadata), so the equation rides along invisibly while the file still plays.

Why the branded .uvme extension over a bare .wav / .uvm: fewer apps grab it by file association, so it survives as a combiner file. It is byte-identical to the .wav we export — rename either way freely. Legacy .uvm and plain .wav still decode.

Ins — what you can put IN (one converter, every input)

Outs — what you can get OUT (the Rosetta extract)

Once you hold the field C, one master function FT_COMBINER.extract(file, kind) (and produce(rec, kind)) solves the equation for whichever representation you ask — nothing is a stored duplicate, each output is derived live from C by its master map (Φimg / Φsnd / Φfrc):

So the chain is: anything → C → anything. Drop a sound, get the image; drop an image, get the sound or the fractal; feed the rebuilt image straight into the viewer's ∞ zoom-fractalize or the world compositor. One field, one converter, every direction.

What each file carries — the full combination. Every file made on this site carries both faces of whatever you put in, so any one file pulls the lot back: an exported .png shows your original image as its visible pixels and carries the equation in a hidden tEXt chunk; an image-source .uvme embeds the original image alongside the equation; a sound-source .uvme/.wav holds your original audio in its data chunk alongside the equation. So on the Combiner, Your-equations and the Viewer you get two live toggles — image: original / equation and sound: original / equation — and ∞ zoom works on whichever picture you're showing. The only hard limit is physics: a .png is an image so it can't play on its own (use the .uvme/.wav for that), and an image-source file has no original sound to toggle to (only the equation's music), just as a sound-source file has no original picture (only the equation render). Gallery pieces ship as equations only (no original scan) — there the toggle shows equation-only.

The two flavours of “equation in a sound” (and why both exist)

  • Music .uvme (exact, data-carried): a listenable song with the byte-exact dense field in the UVME chunk. Rebuilds the original image exactly. The audible music itself is not reversible — it's a lossy artistic projection; the exact equation rides as data alongside it.
  • Equational sound (the waveform IS the equation): a 16-tone MFSK modem encodes the compact field (dims + palette + strongest coefficients) as pure tones a Goertzel demodulator reads straight back — no data chunk. Deterministic + CRC-checked over lossless PCM. Rebuilds an exact-to-the-compact-field image (a real, slightly-abstracted version of the dense original).

Why not one file that's both exact and short? A byte-exact dense field is megabytes; encoding that “as listenable sound” of any sane length is information-theoretically impossible (it'd be hours of audio or raw-data screech). So the split is honest, not a limitation we hid: exact → the data-carried music .uvme; compact-but-self-carrying → the equational sound.

Motion — the .uvmv sibling, and .gif / .mp4 as equations

The same idea extends to motion on the Equational Video page: a clip becomes a sequence of per-frame equations {Ct} + its audio + fps, packed into a .uvmv container. It reconstructs & plays from the maths and exports a .webm, an .mp4, or an animated .gif.

.gif and .mp4 are first-class equational citizens, all directions (sound↔gif↔mp4↔image). An animated GIF decodes to per-frame equations (and re-exports as one); an mp4/webm the same; an image or sound blooms into an equational zoom-dive video (the infinite Newton/N³ fractalize, baked frame-by-frame) — for a sound you pick the track: the equation-music, the original, or none. From any motion file you can also pull the four answers: a still image (carrying its equation), the original sound (if the clip had one — a gif doesn’t), the music of the equation, and the equational images. Beyond Eq Video, you can drop your own gif/mp4/image/sound straight onto the world engine to morph it in the compositor, and the Combiner / Your-equations / Viewer play a dropped animated gif/video instead of freezing to one frame.

Honest scope: v1 keeps a compact field per frame (inter-frame delta is a documented next step). In-browser .mp4 uses the browser’s H.264 recorder (Chrome 126+/Edge/Safari) — elsewhere export .webm or use the desktop Converter (Rust ffmpeg) for a guaranteed .mp4. The .webm/.gif play everywhere; the .uvmv is the exact-equation file.

Built to grow (extensible & versioned)

The stone is meant to expand: new combiner equations (more transforms of C — new things to discover) become new produces entries + a produce() branch, and any algorithm update rides the uvm-combiner@N schema version stamped in the chunk — bump the version, teach the decoder the new one, and every older .uvme still decodes. Forward-compatible by design. The byte-level container layout, the three sound paths, the plays-where matrix and every symbol are detailed in the complete reference.

These are equations, not images

Plain version. A normal gallery stores pictures — JPGs or PNGs, grids of saved pixels. This one stores none. Every artwork has been converted into a recipe of mathematics: a long list of waves (wavelets), each with a strength and a place, plus colour rules. When you open a piece, your browser solves that recipe and draws the picture from scratch. Turn the deviation dial and you literally add or remove equation terms and watch the image rebuild; download the full equation and you get every term as text. The picture is an output of the maths, not a stored file.

Technical version. Each template is a sparse, multi-level separable CDF 9/7 biorthogonal wavelet expansion (the JPEG2000 wavelet) per YCbCr channel: channel(x,y) = Σ_k c_k · ψ_k(x,y), where each ψ is a 9/7 basis function at a given scale and position and c_k is its coefficient. The analyzer (OpenCV/NumPy) forward-transforms each image at true native resolution (the source long edge, padded to a multiple of 64 — not a power of two, which keeps the grid tight), then keeps the coefficients needed to hit a per-channel L2 fidelity target. Because that target is an energy threshold, not a fixed cap, a complex image carries a genuinely different term count than a simple one (here from ~0.4M for the sparsest piece up to ~5M for the densest, with most pieces in the millions) — every image is its own equation, not a fixed-length list. Coefficients ship compactly: LEB128 delta-varint positions (sorted, so deltas pack to ~1 byte) + Int16 quantized values × a per-channel scale, base64 — never pixels. The browser runs the matching 9/7 inverse transform (verified byte-for-byte identical to the analyzer) to reconstruct at native resolution and aspect. Colour is carried exactly in YCbCr; the title caption is inpainted out before analysis so no equation encodes text. Reconstruction fidelity is reported per image as PSNR.

TL;DR: no image files exist here — each picture is a live solution of its own equations (thousands of wavelet terms + colour formulas), reconstructed in your browser.

Overview

Uni Vs Matics scans a corpus of fractal art and reduces each qualifying image to a mathematical template. A template carries three forms of the same image's math: a structured numeric attribute record, a set of human-readable symbolic equations, and a generative IFS that regenerates a fractal in the source's family. Images that don't yield useful, unique equations — fog/blur, TV-snow static, text/emoji-dominant frames, or near-duplicates — are filtered out automatically.

About the source art

The corpus is a single artist's body of work: a schizophrenic artist with photographic memory who recalled the hallucinations they experienced and drew them, by hand, exactly as they saw them. These are not procedurally-generated fractals — they are remembered visions, hand-rendered from perfect recall. Uni Vs Matics exists to capture the underlying mathematics of those recalled forms, so the structures can be studied, browsed, reconstructed, morphed, and even heard.

Source: the original hand-drawn gallery — GFourteen on DeviantArt.

Every template traces back to one of these recalled works. The reject ladder is tuned to honour them — a small title caption never disqualifies a piece, and only genuine fog, static, or text-dominant frames are set aside.

The pipeline

  1. Load & guard — raster only; animated gif/apng/webp and non-images are skipped. Images are analyzed at a bounded resolution (default 1024 px on the long edge).
  2. Fog / blur reject — Laplacian variance, Canny edge density, and high-frequency spectral energy. Smooth, foggy, vector-less images are dropped.
  3. TV-snow static reject — luminance entropy, spectral flatness, and lag-1 autocorrelation. White-noise-like "snow" is dropped.
  4. Vector extraction — unsharp mask → Canny → probabilistic Hough → colinear "same-trajectory" decimation, plus a structure-tensor orientation field. The long-line count becomes a structure score.
  5. Text / emoji reject — MSER glyph-row analysis with uniformity, spacing-regularity and a dominance gate: only a text-filled frame is rejected. Strong line-vector structure vetoes the reject, so a small title caption never disqualifies vectored art.
  6. Colour, lumen, waveform — palette + per-channel GMMs; luminance field, density aggregation, degradation-propagation field, morph-allotment gradients; radial/angular FFT spectra + power-law β.
  7. Generative IFS — self-similarity collage fit → affine maps + weights + variation tag + box-counting dimension.
  8. Quality gate — an intricacy score (vector richness, edge density, spectral structure, luminance range) must clear a floor.
  9. Corpus uniqueness — feature-vector cosine dedup; near-duplicate equation signatures are dropped, highest-intricacy representative kept.
The reject ladder is deliberately conservative about text: this gallery's fractals all carry one/two-word title captions, so a localized caption is ignored and the engine grabs the main focal fractal.

Two engines

The same mathematics drives two completely different experiences. Neither is a slideshow and neither stores pixels.

1 · Exact-copy engine (the viewer)

Plain version. Open any piece and your browser rebuilds the actual artwork from its equations at full native resolution — drag to pan, scroll to zoom, turn the deviation dial to add or drop equation terms and watch the picture rebuild, or download every term as text. Prev/next buttons and the ←/→ keys scroll through every reconstruction in the corpus, in-place, without leaving the viewer.

Math-layer still. Alongside the 2D reconstruction the viewer shows a math-layer still of the piece — the same flat layered compositor as the world engine, pinned to this one piece. Its math layers (the reconstruction plus glow, structure, colour field, edges and fine detail) are stacked and blended flat, geometry locked to the frame (portrait turned on its side). Open the video page for the full live, morphing version.

Live morph — the "3D universe" inside the viewer. Hit live morph (or play) and the viewer builds a ladder of real reconstructions across the equation's term-set and continuously cross-fades through them, so the main image equation visibly morphs as its active terms emerge and dissolve. When you play the equation as music, the morph follows the music's progress — the full equation plays out in picture and sound together.

∞ Zoom-fractalize. Scroll to zoom into the reconstruction — it stays the real image until you pass its native detail, then it fractalizes: the piece's own equation (complex Newton z³−K + the cubic x³−2y³=3, with sine/cosine navigators warping all axes) grows new self-similar detail in the piece's palette colours, propagating out of the image's own edges and vectors (Sobel-masked + additively composited, so the filigree grows ALONG the structure — not a fade pasted over the top). The micro-detail frequency rides the zoom, so it refines forever and never bottoms out, drifting into fresh equational territory as you go deeper. All solved live from the maths — never a stored or tiled image.

The equation as music (the master music equation, not a drone). The sound is a pure function of the image's own wavelet coefficients — no scale, key, melody or rhythm is added ahead of time (that would be a gimmick). Each coefficient's wavelet band sets its octave, so coarse structure plays as bass and fine detail as treble — a wide register straight from the maths (~40 Hz to ~3 kHz). The strongest few coefficients per band are voiced (so every octave speaks and each image sounds different), magnitude sets loudness, and they're read out in a stride-rotated round-robin across the bands so the melody leaps across octaves rather than climbing a low→high scale (that ascending sweep sounded like a strobe). Every note is a real pluck/pad with harmonics + envelope — never a buzz. In the world engine a continuous scheduler crossfades one piece's note-set into the next by the live morph amount, so the music morphs and merges without pauses. The exact formulas are on the equations page (form 9) so anyone can recreate the same music from a template. One plug-in turns any equation (gallery or your own upload) into music.

2 · World engine (the video page)

Plain version. A living world built from the equations — not the pictures, and not a camera flying around a 3D image. Each piece is split into its math layers and those layers are stacked flat, blended, and slowly merged into the next piece. The maths of the image is what's on screen; portrait pieces are turned on their side to fill the frame.

Technical version. A flat layered math compositor. From each piece's equation we decompose seven math layers — recon (the full faithful reconstruction, the base), base (coarse glow), mid (mid-frequency structure), colour (the spatial colour-field wash), lumen (luminance density), edges (Canny line structure) and spark (high-frequency filament detail) — all at native resolution. The engine draws the reconstruction as the base, then stacks the enriching layers on top with their own blend modes (screen / lighter / overlay / soft-light) and filters, with the geometry locked to the frame edges (no drift, no sway, no camera). Whole composites cross-fade piece→piece for the evolving, merging world. "3D" in the compositor is the depth of the compositing stack, not perspective. (The viewer's ∞ zoom-fractalize — mode E — solves the equation across three axes; see section C2.)

  • Merge — whole-composite cross-fade builds one piece's math layers into the next; nothing is a slideshow.
  • Fill the screen, locked — exact cover-fit pinned to the frame edges for every piece; portrait pieces rotated 90° (width→height) and cover-cropped; fullscreen-able.
  • Transport — play / pause, speed, morph length, layer enrichment, plus a playlist (48 / 120 / all) with loop & shuffle.
  • Full effects palette — every blend mode (with a global override), the CSS filter set (hue-rotate, blur, brightness, saturation, contrast, sepia, invert, grayscale), per-layer on/off toggles, and an evolve control that makes the layers build, collapse and merge (and the hue drift) over time — all without moving the locked geometry.
  • ⟳ reset view — returns the playlist to the start. (Zoom-into-the-image & fractalize lives on the viewer, where a single still can be zoomed into infinitely.)
  • Native detail — layers scanned at native resolution; rendered up to 1920px — no shit-res math capture.
  • Capture — PNG or JPG of the exact hi-res frame; record the live world to .webm.
  • GIF (512²) — the last few seconds from a rolling buffer, with an optional seamless boomerang loop. Encoded in a Web Worker so playback never stutters.
  • Silent saves — pick a folder once (File System Access API) and every PNG/JPG/GIF/webm writes there with no Save-As prompt.

3 · Your own equations (in-browser, on Your equations)

Plain version. Upload any image and it is turned into its own equation right in your browser — the exact same CDF 9/7 maths as the gallery. Nothing is sent to a server. Your equations are saved in your browser (so they persist between visits), and you can reconstruct, deviation-dial, live-morph and sonify them just like a gallery piece.

Technical version. assets/equationalize.js runs the forward CDF 9/7 lifting transform (a verified machine-exact inverse-pair of the shipped decoder), selects coefficients to an energy target so each image gets its own honest term count, quantises to Int16 and packs positions with the same LEB128 delta-varint scheme the corpus uses — producing a reconstruction object byte-compatible with the viewer. Persisted to IndexedDB; entirely client-side, no upload, no dependency.

These are equations — not images (so the engine won't behave like one)

Because every picture is a live solution of its equations, the rendering engines (viewer, world engine, your-equations) block right-click “Save image as” / copy and drag-to-save on purpose — there is no stored image to grab, only the maths. The real, intended exports are the equation itself (download full equation in the viewer) and explicitly-rendered PNG / JPG / 512² GIF / webm from the world engine's capture buttons. Those go through deliberate handlers, not the image context-menu.

Complete reference — every variable, equation & formula

Every formula the engine uses, with every symbol defined. The same forms are rendered as typeset maths on the equations page; here each is spelled out in full so nothing is left implicit. (Notation: Σ = sum, ⌊·⌋ = floor/round-down, |·| = absolute value, √ = square root.)

The combiner — one shared variable space (the master of masters)

Every transform below reads ONE shared state — the wavelet coefficient field. The image, the sound and the fractal are all functions of the SAME C, which is what makes them inter-convertible (“equatable between master equations”) and what lets the reverse features run.

C = { (rk, ck, vk) : k = 1..N } (+ palette P, dims W×H) Φimg(C) = Σ vk · ψ(rk,ck) (→ the image) Φsnd(C) = ( ℓk, fk, ak, tk ) (→ the sound) Φfrc(C) = { Ak, tk, wk } ; grow = iterate x³−2y³=3 (→ the fractal)

Why exact + listenable can’t be the same file (no smoke): a byte-exact dense field is megabytes; encoding that “as sound” of any listenable length is information-theoretically impossible (it would be hours of audio, or raw-data noise — which is exactly why the music .uvme tacks the data on). The rebuilt image then feeds the engines — the viewer’s zoom-fractalize or the world compositor — so the chain is sound → equation C → image → engine (the dimensional / fractal representation). Any result can be saved as .uvme, .wav, an equational sound, or a plain .png (re-uploadable for more conversions). Try the example sound on the Combiner page to watch a found sound become an image.

What's playable, what converts — and the one converter that bridges it

The sounds really are sounds. A .uvme (and a .wav exported here, and an equational sound) is a genuine, standard RIFF/WAVE file — it plays in any normal audio app. The .uvme just carries the equation in an extra UVME chunk that audio players skip as unknown (the same trick broadcast WAV uses for its bext metadata); the equational sound carries it in the audible tones themselves. We use the branded .uvme extension (over a bare .wav/.uvm) so other apps are less likely to grab it by file association — rename it to .wav any time you just want to play it.

About images / .png / .jpg: the exported .png also carries the equation — hidden in a standard tEXt chunk (PNG is chunk-based like WAV). So it shows as a normal picture in any viewer (the visible pixels are the image; the equation is invisible metadata viewers ignore) and re-imports here to rebuild the exact image. What it can't do is play audio — it's an image file. (.jpg/.webp stay plain pixels → re-equationalized fresh.) A single file's magic bytes still pick one type: a .uvme is audio (plays), a .png is an image (shows) — each can secretly carry the equation, but neither both-plays-and-shows.

One unifying converter. Every input above is handled by a single entry point, FT_COMBINER.fromAnyFile(file) — drop an image or any sound and it returns the same field C (auto-routing image→forward-transform, .uvme→chunk, equational-sound→demod, plain-sound→additive). Each .uvme is a Rosetta Stone. Nothing is a redundant stored copy — each output is derived live from C by its master equation (Φimg / Φsnd / Φfrc). Extensible & versioned. The stone is meant to grow: new combiner equations (more transforms of C — new things to discover) become new produces entries + a produce() branch, and any algorithm update rides the uvm-combiner@N schema version stamped in the chunk — bump the version, teach the decoder the new one, and every older .uvme still decodes. Forward-compatible by design.

A · The image equation (reconstruction — what redraws the picture)

channel(x,y) = Σk ck · ψk(x,y) (computed separately for Y, Cb, Cr) R = Y + 1.402 (Cr−½) G = Y − 0.344 (Cb−½) − 0.714 (Cr−½) B = Y + 1.772 (Cb−½)

symbolwhat it is
channel(x,y)The value of one colour channel at pixel column x, row y. Three channels (Y, Cb, Cr) are reconstructed, then converted to RGB.
x, yPixel coordinates, from 0 to the image's native width/height.
kIndex of one term (one wavelet). The sum runs over all kept terms.
NThe number of kept terms in that channel (the equation_count); ranges here from ~0.4M to ~5M depending on the piece's complexity.
ckThe coefficient — how strongly wavelet k contributes (its weight in the recipe).
ψk(x,y)The basis function — one separable CDF 9/7 biorthogonal wavelet (the JPEG2000 wavelet) at a particular scale and position. “Separable” = a 1-D wavelet applied across rows then down columns.
Y, Cb, CrLuminance (brightness) + two chrominance (colour) channels. Storing colour this way matches how the eye works and keeps the maths compact. The RGB formulas above convert back for display.
lifting schemeHow the 9/7 wavelet is computed: alternating predict and update steps plus a scaling constant, applied over multiple dyadic levels (each level is half the size of the previous). It is exactly invertible, so the browser's inverse is byte-for-byte identical to the analyzer's forward transform.
level / scaleWhich dyadic band a wavelet lives in. Low levels = big, coarse shapes; high levels = small, fine detail.
pad_w, pad_hThe working grid width/height — the native size padded up to a multiple of 64 so the multi-level transform divides cleanly.
L2 energy target / fidelityThe rule for how many terms to keep: enough to capture a set fraction of the image's total “energy” (sum of squared values). It's a quality threshold, not a fixed count — so a busy image honestly keeps more terms than a simple one.
PSNRPeak signal-to-noise ratio (decibels) — how faithful the reconstruction is to the original. Higher = closer.
qscale, Int16, dv1 / LEB128How the coefficients ship: values quantised to 16-bit integers times a per-channel scale (qscale), and their positions stored as LEB128 delta-varints (the gaps between sorted positions, packed to ~1 byte each). Base64-encoded text — never pixels.

B · The master music equation (sound — what turns a picture into a song)

ℓk = ⌊ log₂ max(rk, ck, 1) ⌋ (which wavelet band the term sits in) fk = flo · (fhi / flo)(ℓk / ℓmax) · (1 + uk/16) (its pitch, in Hz) ak = |ck| / maxj |cj| (its loudness, 0..1) tk = (rankk / N) · T (when it plays)

symbolwhat it is
ℓkThe wavelet band (scale level) of term k — coarse shapes have a low ℓ, fine detail a high one.
rk, ckThe term's row and column in the coefficient grid (its position). (Note: here ck means column; in section A the same letter meant the coefficient value — context distinguishes them.)
fkThe pitch (frequency in Hz) the term is played at. Band → octave, so coarse structure is bass and fine detail is treble.
flo, fhiThe low and high ends of the pitch range: flo = 40 Hz, fhi = 3200 Hz — a wide register, straight from the maths.
ℓmax⌊ log₂ max(W,H) ⌋ — the deepest band, used to normalise ℓk to 0..1 so the pitch spans the whole range.
ukThe fractional position inside the band (0..1) — a small detune so notes in the same band aren't identical.
akLoudness, the term's magnitude divided by the largest magnitude — stronger coefficients play louder.
tkOnset time — when the note plays, from its position rankk in the read-out order across the total duration T.
rankk (read-out order)The order the notes are played in. The strongest few coefficients per band are chosen, then read out in a stride-rotated round-robin across the bands (stride ≈ half the band count, starting band drifting each round) so the melody leaps across octaves instead of climbing a low→high scale — that ascending sweep was an audible strobe. No scale, key or tune is added; only the read-out order changes.
TThe clip length (seconds) the note-set is laid out over; in the world engine the stream is continuous and crossfades piece→piece.
stereo panLeft/right placement, taken from the dominant palette hue at the note's column — colour → stereo image.

C · The generative IFS (fractal regeneration) + the ∞ zoom-fractalize

xn+1 = Ak xn + tk , Ak = [ a b ; c d ] , tk = [ e ; f ] , Pr(choose k) = wk 3D depth lift: zn+1 = √|det Ak| · zn + shellk box-counting dimension: D = limε→0 log N(ε) / log(1/ε)

symbolwhat it is
IFS / chaos gameIterated function system: pick one map at random (by its weight), apply it to the current point, plot, repeat. The cloud of plotted points fills the attractor — a true fractal in the artwork's family.
Ak = [a b; c d]The 2×2 linear part of map k — it rotates, scales and shears the point.
tk = [e; f]The translation part — it shifts the point. (a,b,c,d,e,f are the six numbers stored per map.)
wkThe probability of choosing map k each step (the maps' weights sum to 1).
xnThe point after n iterations; the orbit of points fills the fractal.
det AkThe determinant ad−bc — how much the map shrinks area — its contraction factor.
zn, shellkDepth terms from an earlier IFS depth-lift experiment. The shipped interactive zoom is mode E (see C2) — it solves the equation across three axes and renders on the 2D plane; it does not use an IFS depth-lift.
DFractal (box-counting) dimension — a number (often non-integer) describing how the detail fills space.
N(ε), εN(ε) = how many boxes of size ε are needed to cover the edge set; the slope of log N vs log(1/ε) as boxes shrink is D.

C2 · The zoom-fractalize "engine" (mode E: Newton→N³) — exactly what it is

Stated honestly, no overclaiming — it is a composite of established methods, driven by each image's own equation; the combination is bespoke, but no single step is a new invention:

  1. Complex stage = the classic NEWTON FRACTAL. Newton–Raphson iteration z ← z − (z³−K)/(3z²) in the complex plane (the fractal of Cayley's 1879 problem). This is 2-dimensional — the complex plane is two real axes. This part is genuinely Newton's, not ours.
  2. Then a real 3D step (this is where it goes beyond 2D Newton). Where Newton converges, a point (x,y,z) is Newton-projected onto the implicit cubic surface x³−2y³−z³=K — step −g·∇g/|∇g|², ∇g=(3x²,−6y²,−3z²). Three real axes participate; the z axis is seeded by the sine/cosine navigators. So the solve is 3-dimensional.
  3. Texture & colour. The result is shaded with cellular (Worley) noise [Steven Worley, 1996] in multi-octave fBm [Mandelbrot/Perlin], the coordinate domain-warped by the sine/cosine navigators, and coloured from the piece's palette by which root-basin it falls in — all seeded by the local visual entropy of the reconstruction, so the growth carries that image's own character. Octaves step by √10 and the noise lattice is decimal, so the scale is base-ten, proportional to the camera's zoom decades.

So is it "a 3D engine"? The maths is solved across three axes (genuinely more than the 2D complex Newton fractal), but the render is a 2D-plane view of that 3D solve — precisely "a 3D representation of the 2D plane," not a navigable 3D scene you fly through. That distinction is the honest one. Provenance: Newton–Raphson (Newton), the Newton fractal (Cayley), implicit-surface projection (standard numerical method), Worley cellular noise, fBm, domain warping — all named prior art; only the equation-driven combination is bespoke.

Notes & limitations

  • The IFS fit is an approximation (self-similarity collage), not an exact flame inversion. Regenerated images are "in the family", not pixel-exact.
